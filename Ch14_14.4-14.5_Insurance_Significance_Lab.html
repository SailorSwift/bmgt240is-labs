STOP 1 · THE BUSINESS QUESTION

Each year of age adds about $258. Is that real, or sampling noise?

Last time Bridger's line was y = 3,165.89 + 257.72x, with r² = 0.089. Before anyone builds age-based prices on it, the pricing team asks: if age had no relationship with charges at all, could a sample like ours still produce a slope of $258?

Same five-step test as Ch 9, new parameter

H0: β1 = 0 (age is not related to charges)
Ha: β1 ≠ 0

β1 is the slope for all of Bridger's customers. b1 = 257.72 is our estimate of it.

Predict first

Age explains only 9% of the variation in charges. With all 1,338 customers, will the test reject H0?

Keep your answer. Stop 5 settles it.

The test only works if the error term ε behaves. So first, §14.4: what are we assuming about ε, and does Bridger's data go along?

Spread is about the same at every age, so assumption 2 holds. But the errors are far from normal: most customers sit a little below the line and a few sit far above it. All 147 residuals more than 2s above the line belong to smokers.

Why we can still test: with n = 1,338, the sampling distribution of b1 is close to normal anyway (the same Central Limit Theorem logic as Ch 7). With 5 customers we could not lean on that. Write this in your report's assumptions section.

STOP 4 · 14.5 · CONFIDENCE INTERVAL FOR THE SLOPE

Significant tells you “not zero.” The interval tells you how big.

b1 ± tα/2 · sb1

5 customers: 267 ± 3.182 × 33.57$160 to $374
All 1,338: 257.72 ± 1.962 × 22.50$214 to $302

The 5-customer interval doesn't contain 0, which matches “reject.” But it's so wide that it's nearly useless for pricing. An interval that excludes 0 is the same conclusion as rejecting H0: β1 = 0.

t = 11.45, p = 4.89 × 10⁻²⁹ · Reject H0, easily

For the pricing team: age is significantly related to annual charges. We're 95% confident each extra year of age adds between $214 and $302. Yet r² is still 0.089: significant and a good predictor are different claims. The test says the slope isn't 0; r² says age leaves 91% of the variation unexplained.

4.89E-29 is 0.0000…0489, with 28 zeros after the decimal point. Report it as p < .0001.

The sentence your final report needs (§6)

“We tested H0: β1 = 0 against Ha: β1 ≠ 0. With t = 11.45 and p < .0001 we reject H0: age is significantly related to annual charges. Each additional year adds between $214 and $302 (95% CI). Age explains 9% of the variation (r² = .089), so Bridger should not price on age alone.”
