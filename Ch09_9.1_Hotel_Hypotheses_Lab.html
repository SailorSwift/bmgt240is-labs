STOP 1 · THE TWO HYPOTHESES

Every test starts with two competing statements

Both are statements about a population parameter, such as the population mean μ or the population proportion p, never about the sample.

Null hypothesis · H0

The hypothesis tentatively assumed true in a hypothesis test.

It is a tentative assumption about the value of a population parameter. We keep it unless the sample gives strong evidence against it.

H0: μ ≤ 24

Textbook Ch 9 key terms; Section 9.1.

Alternative hypothesis · Ha

The hypothesis concluded to be true if H0 is rejected.

It is the opposite of what is stated in the null hypothesis. It is usually what the test is trying to find evidence for.

Ha: μ > 24

Textbook Ch 9 key terms; Section 9.1.

An everyday version: a jury trial

H0: the defendant is innocent. The jury starts by assuming it.
Ha: the defendant is guilty. It needs strong evidence.

A guilty verdict rejects H0. A “not guilty” verdict does not prove innocence; the evidence just was not strong enough.

Quick check · true or false?
STOP 2 · THE BOOK'S TWO STARTING POINTS

Start from what the test is trying to show

Two questions to ask first: What is the purpose of collecting the sample? What conclusion are we hoping to make?

Start with Ha · a research hypothesis

Fuel injection. A car gets 24 mpg in city driving. Engineers built a new fuel-injection system and want statistical support that it gets more than 24 mpg.

H0: μ ≤ 24
Ha: μ > 24

What they hope to show goes in Ha. H0 is the cautious assumption: the new system is no better.

Same pattern: a new teaching method, a new bonus plan, a new drug. Rejecting H0 supports the new idea.

Start with H0 · an assumption to be challenged

Soft-drink label. A bottle says 67.6 fluid ounces. We give the manufacturer the benefit of the doubt: the label is correct if the mean fill is at least 67.6.

H0: μ ≥ 67.6
Ha: μ < 67.6

The claim or belief goes in H0. Ha is the challenge: bottles are underfilled.

From the manufacturer's view, under- and overfilling both matter, so the same bottles give H0: μ = 67.6, Ha: μ ≠ 67.6.

The hypotheses depend on whose point of view you take and what that person needs to find out.

STOP 6

What can you conclude?

Test resultWhat it meansSay it like thisDo not say
Reject H0The sample gives strong evidence for Ha.“There is evidence that μ > 600.”“We proved μ > 600.”
Cannot reject H0The sample does not give strong enough evidence for Ha.“We cannot conclude that μ > 600.”“We proved μ ≤ 600” or “We accept H0.”
Why “cannot reject” is not “true”

A jury that finds a defendant “not guilty” has not proved innocence. It found the evidence was not strong enough. H0 is the starting assumption, kept until the evidence says otherwise.

Remember

The equality always goes in H0. What you are looking for evidence of usually goes in Ha. Rejecting H0 is the only way to reach a strong conclusion.
